# Sample Batch 01 — written Aug 26, 2026

Every rewrite below was written from that brand's own public product copy,
ingredients, and origin story. Nothing invented. This is the free work that
goes out attached to the first email.

---

## 1. Salce — Queen Creek, AZ · jared@getsalce.com · Jared Beauchamp
**Target product:** Pineapple Jalapeño
**Their current copy:** ingredient list + "a flavor-first sauce that blends
various peppers with the sweetness of fruit or honey."

**Rewrite:**

> It started on a grill in 2018. Jalapeños straight off the stem. Pineapple,
> charred until the edges went dark and sweet. Blended together because it
> tasted better than anything sitting in the fridge door.
>
> That's still the whole recipe — pineapple, jalapeño, white onion, garlic,
> a squeeze of lemon. Nothing to mask. Nothing to thicken.
>
> It goes on eggs, tacos, wings, and roughly half of whatever comes off your
> grill this summer. Heat you can finish a whole bottle of. Sweetness that
> doesn't apologize for itself.
>
> Handmade in small batches in Queen Creek, Arizona.

**The craft note (goes in the email):** their real origin story — grilled
pineapple, 2018 — was buried on a separate page while the product page led
with an ingredient list. The story *is* the differentiator; it belongs where
the buy button is.

## 2. Cooper's Small Batch — Denver, CO · via site contact form
**Target product:** Leche Diablesa
**Their current copy:** "a special white hot sauce with spicy heat paired with
creamy coconuts and lime." Positioning: chef-made, milder, built to
complement food rather than overpower it.

**Rewrite:**

> Most hot sauce is a dare. This one is an ingredient.
>
> Leche Diablesa is a white sauce — coconut, lime, and a clean habanero heat
> that arrives late and leaves politely. It doesn't bury what you put it on.
> It finishes it. Fish tacos, grilled shrimp, roasted sweet potato, anything
> that wants brightness and a little danger.
>
> Made by a chef in Denver, in batches small enough to taste every one.
> All natural, gluten free, no preservatives, no thickeners — a sauce built
> on real ingredients doesn't need help standing up.

**The craft note:** their whole positioning is "milder, made to pair with
food, not to punish you" — and that argument never appears on the product
page. The opening line makes it the first thing a buyer reads.

## 3. HAB Sauce — Portland, OR · via site
**Target product:** Smoked Cherrywood
**Their current copy:** "Tomatillos, habaneros and garlic gently smoked with
cherrywood with white vinegar and himalayan salt."

**Rewrite:**

> We smoke the tomatillos before anything else happens.
>
> Cherrywood, low and slow, until the habaneros and garlic pick up something
> you cannot fake with liquid smoke or a spice jar. Then white vinegar, a
> little pink Himalayan salt, and nothing else at all. That's the sauce.
>
> It's the bottle people buy for brisket and end up keeping next to the eggs.
> Smoke first, heat second — the habanero shows up at the end and stays a
> while.
>
> Small batch, Portland, Oregon. Made by the same people who drew the label.

**The craft note:** the ingredient line already contains the best sentence on
the page — "gently smoked with cherrywood" — and it's written like a
compliance label. Leading with the process turns a spec into a reason to buy.

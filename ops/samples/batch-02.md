## 2. Beth's Farm Kitchen
**To:** `bfk@bethsfarmkitchen.com` (published on bethsfarmkitchen.com/pages/contact-us)
**⚠ DO NOT ADDRESS AS "BETH."** Beth Linskey sold the business in 2016. It is run
by Guillermo Maciel and Jodie Emmett. Getting this wrong is an instant tell.
**T1c hook line:**
> "Beth started this in her own kitchen in 1981. The couple who bought it in 2016
> are farmers — and they're adding dairy from their own flock of sheep."

**Why:** the handover is the most interesting thing about the brand and the
About page treats it as a footnote.

**The ~100 words, held in reserve:**

Beth Linskey started making jam in her Stuyvesant Falls kitchen in 1981. Over the
next 35 years her jars became a fixture at the Union Square Greenmarket, and she
wrote many of the recipes still in use today.

In 2016 she sold the kitchen to us — Guillermo and Jodie, farmers before we were
jam makers. We kept her recipes and her sourcing, and added organic salsa, BBQ
sauce, ketchup and mustard, all from Hudson Valley produce.

Next: farmstead dairy from our own flock of sheep.

Same kitchen. Same valley. Still small batch.

*Sources: bethsfarmkitchen.com/pages/about-us; Edible Manhattan profile
(founding 1981, Greenmarket, 2016 sale to Maciel/Emmett, Old Chatham facility,
product line, planned sheep dairy).*

## 4. Smart Cookie Barkery
**To:** `woof@smartcookietreats.com` (published on smartcookietreats.com)
**T1c hook line:**
> "You sold your first treats out of a wooden tricycle. You're in four hundred
> stores now."

**Why the tricycle and not the diagnosis:** their founding story involves their
dog's lymphoma and Bri's breast cancer at 24. It is published by them, on their
own About page, so it is fair to work with — but leading a cold email with a
stranger's cancer would be mining it. The tricycle is just as true, just as
arresting, and doesn't trade on illness. The diagnosis appears in the long-form
rewrite, in their own voice, where it belongs.

**The ~100 words, held in reserve:**

In 2012 our Aussie was diagnosed with lymphoma. That same year Bri, then 24, was
diagnosed with breast cancer.

We got obsessive about nutrition — what's actually in food, and what it does.
Then we started baking for dogs, loaded the treats onto a wooden tricycle, and
sold them on the street.

That tricycle is still around. So are we, in roughly 400 independent pet shops.

Single protein. Limited ingredients. Made from scratch in a real kitchen in
Golden, Colorado, by people you could come and meet.

*Sources: smartcookietreats.com/pages/about-us; VoyageDenver interview with Bri
Bradley; PetfoodIndustry distribution report (2012 founding, both diagnoses,
Smart Cookie Cart tricycle, ~400 independent pet specialty stores, single-protein
limited-ingredient line, Golden CO).*

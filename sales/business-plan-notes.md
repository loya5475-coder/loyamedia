# Business Plan Notes — My Honest Input on the Model
### Where the plan is strong, where to fix it, and the numbers

## The verdict up front

The model is solid. Blue-collar + no-website + border region is a genuinely underserved market: these owners have money (average job $3k–$20k), they know they're invisible online, and almost nobody is calling them in Spanish with a finished demo in hand. Your two structural advantages are **you're local** and **AI makes the build nearly free**. The plan needs five upgrades:

---

## 1. Biggest upgrade: sell demo-first, always

Your current plan builds the website *after* they agree to a meeting. Flip it: **build the demo BEFORE the first cold call.** With landingsite.ai it costs you ~15 minutes per business. Now your cold call isn't "can I build you a website" (a pitch), it's "I already built your website, want to see it?" (a curiosity bomb). You can text the link straight to their phone mid-call.

- Describing a website closes ~10% of appointments. Showing a finished site with THEIR name and THEIR reviews closes 30–50%.
- It also fixes your appointment no-show problem before it starts — they've already seen the thing; the meeting is just the close.
- Practical: each evening, build demos for the next day's 10–15 call targets. Reviews + services come off their Google listing (that's the "context" you mentioned — you had this instinct right).

## 2. Pricing: $800 is fine, but anchor higher and never say it first

- **Don't open with $800.** Open with the anchor: "Agencies charge $2,500–$5,000 for this" (true), then land at $800 as your portfolio-building local rate. Same number, totally different feeling.
- Consider **$800 / $1,200 two-tier**: Standard (the site) vs. Pro (site + Google Business Profile optimization + review setup). Many will take the $1,200 — people buy the middle-to-top option — and $800 now feels like the budget choice instead of the ask.
- **$50/mo is right.** Market rate for "hosting + a guy who answers texts" is $50–$150. Don't go lower; raise it to $75 for new clients once you have 10.
- Take 50% deposit at yes, balance at handoff. Never build on a handshake.
- One discount you should happily give: **$100 off for a referral that closes.** Cheapest marketing you'll ever do.

## 3. The real business is the $50/mo — protect it

The $800 is income. The $50/mo is a **company**:

| Clients | One-time (@$800) | Recurring/yr (@$50/mo) |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | $8,000 | $6,000/yr |
| 30 | $24,000 | $18,000/yr |
| 50 | $40,000 | $30,000/yr |

- Set up **auto-billing** (PayPal subscription / Stripe payment link) at handoff — never invoice monthly by hand, never let them "just pay you when they see you."
- Keep all domains in one Loya Media registrar account with auto-renew (see delivery playbook). You manage everything = they have no reason to leave; you transfer the domain freely if they ask = you keep your reputation in a small market. Both things at once.
- Churn killer: same-day small edits + a Day-30 "your site ranks #2 for X" text. Clients cancel hosting they never think about; they keep hosting that texts them good news.

## 4. Fix the funnel math expectations (so you don't quit in week 2)

Realistic cold-call funnel in this market:

- 20 dials/day → 6–8 real conversations → 1–2 demos texted/booked
- 10 demos shown → 3–5 closes (demo-first)
- **So: ~100 dials/week ≈ 2–5 clients/week.** That's $1,600–$4,000/wk one-time plus compounding $50/mo. The model works at boring, consistent volume — most people quit after 40 dials and one rude roofer. Also: most closes happen on follow-up call 3–5, not call 1. The follow-up cadence in the sales sheet IS the job.
- The spreadsheet's Call Status column is your CRM for now. When you pass ~50 active leads, move to a free CRM (HubSpot free / Google Sheets with reminders) so follow-ups never slip.

## 5. Risks & rules (cheap insurance)

- **Call from your cell, one lead at a time, and honor "take me off your list."** Manual one-to-one calls to business numbers are the right side of the rules; robo-dialing/mass-SMS is not. You're fine — stay fine.
- **Re-verify before dialing:** numbers in the sheet come from public listings; a 5-second Google of the name catches disconnects and stale listings.
- **Don't promise rankings.** Promise a professional site, live in a week, that shows up when people search their NAME, and improves over time for trade searches. Under-promise, screenshot-over-deliver.
- **landingsite.ai dependency:** you're building your recurring revenue on their platform. It's fine to start, but know your exit: if they raise prices or degrade, you can rebuild any of these single-page sites on your own hosting (you already hand-build sites — this repo proves it). Keep each client's photos/copy backed up in a folder so migration is a weekend, not a crisis.
- **Se habla español** is a real moat in El Paso/Doña Ana County. Put it in your pitch, on their sites, and on YOUR site.

## Upsell ladder (once the base model runs)

1. **Google Business Profile optimization** — $150–$250 one-time. Categories, photos, service areas, posts. Biggest visibility bang in local trades; takes you an hour.
2. **Review engine** — $50 setup or bundle into Pro: a QR-code card their crew hands customers → straight to their Google review page. More reviews → they rank higher → your Day-30 screenshot looks great → they never cancel.
3. **Lead texting** — form fills forward to your system, you text the owner "NEW LEAD: Maria, roof leak, 575-xxx" — feels like magic, costs you nothing, justifies $75/mo.
4. **A second town page** ("Roofing in Deming") — $150/page, ranks in zero-competition small towns.
5. Later: simple Google/Facebook ads management (10 clients × $200/mo management fee is a second business).

## Your first 2 weeks, concretely

1. **Days 1–2:** Take the spreadsheet, pick the 20 best leads (high rating + many reviews + "None" presence). Build demos for the top 10 in landingsite.ai.
2. **Day 3:** Start dialing. 20/day. Log every call.
3. **First close:** even if you have to do $500 for client #1 — the first live site + first Google review + first referral is worth more than the $300 you left on the table. (Client #2 pays full price. Client #1's site is your proof.)
4. **Every handoff:** ask for the review + the referral. By client #5, referrals + "seen your work" should be half your pipeline and cold calls become the top-up, not the whole diet.
5. Put a "Websites for Contractors — El Paso & Las Cruces" section/page on loyamedia.com with the demo screenshots, so when a lead Googles YOU mid-call (they will), you look like exactly the guy who does this.
